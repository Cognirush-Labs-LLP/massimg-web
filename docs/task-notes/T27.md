# T27 — PNG path

**Status:** complete

## Headline findings for later tasks

- **The R-Q11 floor of 70 should not move, and the evidence says so twice.** Empirically, the highest
  quantization quality libimagequant can actually reach is **99 or 100 on nine of the ten generated
  PNGs** and **80 to 100 on thirteen of the fourteen real files**; only two of twenty-four images fall
  below 70 (a synthetic photograph at 63, a 32×32 real PNG at 47). Structurally, it cannot produce
  banding at all: R-Q11's mapped value is libimagequant's **`minimum`**, and an image it cannot bring
  up to that quality is *aborted*, not degraded — the job then keeps its lossless PNG. Looking at the
  slider-10 output of a sky gradient, a grey ramp, a shaded illustration and a UI screenshot shows no
  banding; the numeric proxy agrees (largest step introduced across a flat source pair: 1 to 8 units
  on the opaque generated subjects, against 4 to 93 for ImageMagick's quantizer at the same colour
  count).
  **The user decides; `docs/functional-design.md` is unedited.** See "The R-Q11 floor" below.
- **D8 `OPEN 40%` → `REFUTED 95%`.** ImageMagick's own quantizer is worse at the same colour count
  (3.5× and 4.4× larger on shaded and gradient content, RMSE an order of magnitude higher) and —
  decisively — **has no quality floor at all**. `image.quantize({ colors })` always produces a file.
  R-Q11's floor is only expressible through libimagequant's abort, so the 347 KB is not a quality
  upgrade, it is the mechanism.
- **`imagequant` is broken in the Vite *dev server* and fine in the production build.** The dep
  optimizer inlines a second copy of its wasm-bindgen glue, so every error the quantizer raises
  arrives as `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'memory')` instead of its own
  message — and `QUALITY_TOO_LOW` *is* a normal outcome here. Fixed with
  `optimizeDeps: { exclude: ['imagequant'] }` in `vite.config.ts`. Anyone adding another
  wasm-bindgen "bundler"-target package should check for the same shape.
- **oxipng runs single-threaded on all three engines**, because `SharedArrayBuffer` cannot be posted
  without cross-origin isolation. So a pool of *N* workers costs *N* oxipng heaps, not *N* × cores.
  B6 `OPEN 85%` → `OPEN 90%` with the measurement.
- **ImageMagick's PNG writer adds metadata to every file it writes** — `cHRM`, `tIME`, three `tEXt`
  timestamps, and a *second copy of the ICC profile* as a `zTXt` "Raw profile type icc". Neither
  `png:exclude-chunk` nor `image.strip()` removes them without removing the `iCCP` too. The pipeline
  rewrites the chunk list itself, which is also what makes R-I17 structural for PNG.
- **Execution order, for whoever picks up the next task:** `docs/tasks.md` now records
  **T27 → T29 → T28 → T30**, because T29 owns the multi-frame representation GIF will reuse. That
  ordering was settled and written down outside this task; T27 neither decided it nor depends on it,
  and this note did not edit the task list.

## The R-Q11 floor — the answer T27 owes

**Recommendation: leave the floor at 70.** Two independent reasons.

**1. It cannot produce banding, by construction.** R-Q11's mapped value is libimagequant's
`minimum`, and `set_quality(minimum, target)` is pngquant's `--quality min-max` including the abort:
"If the minimum quality can't be met, the quantization will be aborted with an error." So the failure
mode the floor exists to prevent — an image quantized past the point where it bands — cannot occur.
The image aborts and the job keeps its lossless PNG instead. **This changes the question.** "Is 70
low enough to band?" is the right question for a quantizer that clamps; for one that aborts, the
floor decides *how many images get quantized at all*, and a floor that is too high costs size rather
than quality.

**2. Almost nothing lands near it anyway.** The harness binary-searches the highest `minimum` each
image can meet (call it Q\*), which is the number the floor is compared against. **Identical on all
three engines, for all twenty-four images:**

| Subject | Q\* |
| --- | --- |
| Screenshot (light), screenshot (dark), soft-alpha logo, shaded illustration, sky gradient, diagram | 99 |
| Hard-alpha logo, flat illustration, grey ramp | 100 |
| Six real photographs at 1600 px | 80, 92, 92, 92, 96, 97 |
| Seven real PNGs | 99, and 100 six times |
| Synthetic photograph (gradient + ±20 sensor noise) | **63** |
| One 32×32 real PNG | **47** |

Twenty-two of twenty-four are at or above 80. The two below 70 abort at the floor and come out
lossless, which is the intended behaviour and not a defect.

**3. Looked at, not only measured.** `verification/t27-png/render.mjs` writes the slider-10 output so
it can be inspected. A sky gradient, a grey ramp, a shaded illustration and a UI screenshot with a
gradient header show **no visible banding** at slider 10. The numeric proxy agrees: on the six opaque
generated subjects that either quantizer changed at all, the largest step introduced between two
adjacent pixels whose source step was at most one unit is **1 to 8** for libimagequant and **4 to 93**
for ImageMagick's quantizer at the same colour count, where the grain is plainly visible. (The
soft-alpha logo is excluded from both ranges: its large figures, 212 and 224, are entirely colour
under near-transparent pixels, where the largest error on a fully opaque pixel is 0 and 1.)

**What would argue for moving it, and does not.** A weaker quantizer would (the original D8 entry
says so) — but the quantizer is libimagequant, not ImageMagick. A floor *lower* than 70 would let
photographs quantize instead of aborting, which is a size argument against P1 and R-Q11's own
reasoning; the entry does not ask for that and neither do I.

## Decisions made

- **Scope, decided by the user before implementation** (recorded here as given): quantization is
  libimagequant's job pngquant-style, `Imagequant.process()` returns a finished PNG, R-Q11's mapped
  value is the `minimum` and an abort falls back to lossless; no new user-facing control; the
  pipeline shape above; pure split as `encode-plan.ts`/`encode.ts` do it; `executeJob` wired and the
  PNG branch removed from `execute.ts`.
- **`target` is 100, measured — and the size half of the case is closer than it first looks.** The
  alternative, `target` set to the mapped value, is what would make the slider change how *hard* an
  image is quantized. Of 40 image and slider combinations, four abort under both settings, and of
  the **36 comparable ones `target: mapped` is smaller on 16, larger on 12, and the identical size
  on 8**. So on size it is ahead more often than not, and an earlier draft of this note read those
  eight ties as losses and claimed otherwise. What decides it is fidelity: mean SSIM 0.9378 against
  0.9543, and it fails worst exactly where banding shows, because asking for fewer colours makes
  libimagequant dither harder to compensate. A grey ramp at slider 10 came out at 46,628 bytes and
  SSIM 0.9365 against 2,231 bytes and 1.0000 — 20.9× larger *and* further from the source — and a
  shaded illustration at 55,336 and 0.9174 against 32,123 and 0.9995. Where `target: mapped` wins it
  is on screenshots, and it pays there too (27,840 bytes at SSIM 0.9910 against 37,765 at 1.0000).
  **The user-visible consequence, stated plainly: below slider 85 the slider changes which images are
  quantized, not how hard.** It moves `minimum`, so raising it makes more images abort to lossless —
  14 of 24 kept a quantized file at slider 10, 10 of 24 at slider 84, and the mean saving fell from
  45.8% to 39.0%. The slider is therefore a safety control on PNG rather than a size dial, which is
  the direction R-Q11 and P1 point.
- **`speed` is 3** (§7.3's row has the table). One of the ten generated images aborts at every
  speed; of the other nine, **six produce the same output size across speeds 1–6** and three differ
  by at most 168 bytes. (Size, not content — the harness records lengths, so "identical" here is not
  a byte comparison, and an earlier draft of this note said "byte-identical on eight of ten", which
  was wrong on both the word and the count.) Time across speeds 2–6 varies by at most 25 ms per
  image, with speed 1 up to 87 ms slower on one. Speeds 8 and 10 are 1.25×–2.26× faster and change
  the output on seven of the nine, not always for the worse — a gradient got *smaller* — which is
  the point: faster is less predictable rather than uniformly worse. What separates 3 from the
  library default of 4 is Q\*: identical at speeds 1 and 3 on all 24 images, one point lower at
  speed 4 on **three** of them (the same three real photographs on all three engines), and never
  higher. Since Q\* is what R-Q11's floor is compared against, a lower ceiling means a few more
  images abort for no reason. 3 is not maximum effort, which §7.3 forbids and which buys nothing
  here.
- **The rounding is `Math.round`, applied at the quantizer boundary and not in `domain/job.ts`.**
  Nearest is the least-distorting reading of "remapped linearly" and leaves R-Q11's three named
  values (10 → 70, 85 → 95, 100 → 100) untouched. It must come *after* the lossless decision: slider
  84 maps to 94.666…, which rounds to 95, and rounding first would have made it lossless one step
  before R-Q12 says. It is necessary rather than cosmetic, because the value crosses into
  WebAssembly, where a fraction is truncated silently rather than rejected.
- **The pipeline rewrites the PNG chunk list itself** rather than asking ImageMagick to. Measured:
  `png:exclude-chunk` and `image.strip()` both remove the `iCCP` along with the metadata, so neither
  can be used. `rewritePngChunks` keeps a whitelist (`IHDR PLTE tRNS IDAT IEND`) plus whatever
  `IccpPlan` says, moves every chunk **whole** — length, type, data and CRC together — and therefore
  never recomputes a CRC and never can get one wrong.
- **`IccpPlan` is three states, not a nullable chunk.** "Carry whatever the encoder wrote", "attach
  exactly this chunk" and "there is no profile" are different instructions, and collapsing the first
  and the third is how an untagged output quietly acquires a profile or a profiled one loses it. The
  same reasoning `frame.ts` applies to orientation and colour.
- **The `iCCP` chunk the quantized candidate gets is ImageMagick's own, lifted whole.** Not rebuilt —
  that would need a deflate implementation — and not round-tripped through ImageMagick, which risks
  re-encoding the palette. D3's "the profile survives ImageMagick's PNG writer" therefore covers both
  candidates.
- **The splice happens before the size comparison.** Otherwise a quantized candidate could win on
  bytes it does not have: a 2.4 KB profile is a large fraction of a small PNG.
- **The splice happens before oxipng.** Measured byte-identical either way, with the profile exact
  either way, so the choice is on other grounds: doing it first means oxipng sees and emits the final
  chunk set, and the bytes we ship are its own output rather than something edited afterwards.
- **`optimiseAlpha` stays off on both branches.** It is worth real bytes — **28,330 → 1,488** on a
  1000×750 frame whose fully transparent field held noise, a 19× saving for a change nobody can see
  — but the lossless branch exists to be lossless, and R-Q12 makes PNG lossless early precisely for
  that reputation. Measured on the same frame: with the option off the decoded pixels match the
  source exactly, visible and hidden alike; with it on they differ by up to 255 under the transparent
  field. On the quantized branch it buys nothing — libimagequant has already collapsed every fully
  transparent pixel onto one palette entry, and oxipng measured the same 1,488 bytes either way.
  **This is a size win being declined on principle, recorded with its numbers so it can be
  overruled.** Both figures are in `results.json` under `effort.optimiseAlpha`, and
  `tests/e2e/png.spec.ts` asserts the invariant the decision protects: on a frame with thousands of
  fully transparent pixels each holding a different colour, the lossless branch changes none of them.
- **PNG joins JPEG and WebP in `unsupportedColour` rather than getting its own copy.** The colour
  question is the same one with the same answer table, and answering it in two places is how the two
  come to disagree. The claim that this build cannot write a PNG `cICP` chunk is verified, not
  assumed: ImageMagick's PNG coder's own chunk vocabulary in the shipped binary is
  `bKGD,caNv,cHRM,eXIf,gAMA,iCCP,iTXt,pHYs,sRGB,tEXt,zCCP,zTXt,date` with no `cICP` (libpng's own
  table has one; the coder does not), and a PNG written with `png:cicp`, `png:cICP`,
  `png:preserve-cICP` and the `heic:` spellings all set is byte-identical to one written with no
  define at all.
- **The `png-quantized` caveat follows the branch, not the slider.** §6.6's table records it for "PNG
  output below a slider value of 85", but the caveat's own words are "PNG was not written
  losslessly" — which is false when R-Q14 keeps the lossless candidate (8 of 24 images) or when the
  quantizer aborted to it (2 of 24). A caveat that says the output is lossy when it is lossless is a
  caveat the user cannot act on (§14.3). This is the one place I read §6.6's table narrowly; it is
  flagged rather than hidden.
- **An abort is recognised by its message, and anything else propagates.** `isQuantizerAbort` matches
  `QUALITY_TOO_LOW` as a substring. Treating *every* throw from `process()` as an abort would swallow
  a real failure — an out-of-memory, a panic — as a silent fallback to lossless. This is exactly why
  the `optimizeDeps` fix matters: without it the message is unreadable and the two cases are
  indistinguishable.
- **A profiled frame whose `iCCP` ImageMagick did not write is a hard failure**, not a silent drop.
  D3 measured that it always does write one, so this path is untested; failing loudly is the right
  direction when the alternative is losing a colour profile quietly (R-I12, P1).
- **`Imagequant.free()` is called exactly once, in a `finally`.** A second call panics with "null
  pointer passed to rust".
- **The harness's generated content is code, not committed bytes**, unlike T23's fixtures: D8's
  method names *categories* of image, and a generator is the reproducible form of "ten representative
  PNGs". The canvas-drawn subjects have their alpha forced to 255 because WebKit's `getImageData`
  returns 254 on an opaque drawing (T26).

## Surprises

- **`imagequant` is silently broken in the Vite dev server, in a way that would have shipped as a
  bug in the tests rather than in the app.** The dep optimizer inlines a copy of `imagequant_bg.js`
  into `node_modules/.vite/deps` while the `.wasm` module's own imports still resolve to the original
  file, so two copies of the glue exist and only one holds the wasm instance. Every error the
  quantizer raises then arrives as `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading
  'memory')` from the copy that never received it, because `__wbindgen_throw` cannot reach the heap
  to read its own message. A production build has exactly one copy (verified by building
  `src/pipeline/worker.ts` as an entry and counting `__wbg_set_wasm`), so this is dev-only — and dev
  is where every e2e test runs. `optimizeDeps: { exclude: ['imagequant'] }` fixes it.
- **libimagequant's `target` is not a size dial, and turning it down can make the file bigger.** The
  binding's own doc says so ("Images with fewer colors are not always smaller, due to increased
  dithering") but the magnitude is startling: a grey ramp at `target: 70` is **21× larger** than at
  `target: 100`, and visibly worse.
- **`minimum` equal to `target` aborts far more often than either alone.** The first spike set both
  to 70 on a smooth gradient and got `QUALITY_TOO_LOW`, which looked like the floor being
  unreachable; it was the quantizer stopping the moment it hit MSE(70) and then landing a hair under
  it. This is the shape of a wrong conclusion about R-Q13 that a careless measurement would reach.
- **ImageMagick writes 6,241 bytes of PNG for an image whose pixel data is 95 bytes**, once a profile
  is attached: `cHRM`, `tIME`, three `tEXt` timestamps and a 3,441-byte `zTXt` duplicate of the
  profile that is already in the 2,381-byte `iCCP`. And neither `png:exclude-chunk` nor `strip()`
  removes them without taking the profile too. Those byte figures are a one-off observation from a
  spike rather than a row in `results.json`; what is guarded is the qualitative claim —
  `tests/e2e/png.spec.ts` writes the same frame with nothing stripped and asserts `tEXt` and `tIME`
  are present there and absent from the shipped file.
- **ImageMagick's SSIM reads the colour under fully transparent pixels.** A hard-alpha logo whose
  visible pixels are *identical* to the source scores 0.6919, and that single row is enough to pull a
  mean SSIM negative across the set. On images with large transparent regions, RMSE and a peak error
  restricted to opaque pixels are the usable metrics and SSIM is not. Recorded in D8.
- **Firefox does not colour-manage an image drawn into a 2D canvas**, in this Playwright build:
  a Display P3 PNG and an untagged one with identical pixels draw identically, while Chromium and
  WebKit convert the tagged one. The e2e asserts both behaviours per engine rather than skipping
  Firefox. It means the "the engine honours the profile" claim is proven on two engines of three.
- **My synthetic photograph is much harder to quantize than any real one.** It aborts at Q\* 63 while
  six real photographs at 1600 px reach 80 to 97. Uniform per-pixel noise is hostile to a palette in
  the same way T02 found it hostile to AVIF; it is a useful worst case and a bad estimator.
- **Firefox dropped two of the fourteen real files on one run and none on the next.** The first full
  run recorded two "Execution context was destroyed" failures on Firefox where Chromium and WebKit
  processed all fourteen; the committed run, from the same script on the same machine, completed
  24/24 on all three engines. Both files are large, so the likely cause is the tab being reloaded
  under memory pressure with three codec heaps live at 1600 px — which makes it intermittent rather
  than fixed, and worth expecting again. The harness records such a failure in `results.json` and
  excludes the row from that engine's summary rather than hiding it.
- **R-Q14 almost never saves bytes on this set.** Where lossless wins, four of eight are *exact ties*
  and the rest win by 12 to 84 bytes. Its real value is that ten of twenty-four images keep a
  genuinely lossless file and no caveat.

## What the next task should know

- **`encodePng(frame, quality)`** from `@/pipeline/stages/png`. `quality` is
  `ResolvedSettings.quality` with the size target ruled out. The result carries `branch`, `branches`,
  `quantizeAborted`, `caveats`, `sizes` and `paletteColors`; only `bytes` and `caveats` reach
  `JobResult`.
- **`png-plan.ts`'s chunk functions are general PNG surgery** and T29 will want them:
  `readPngChunks`, `readPngHeader`, `readPaletteSize`, `hasAlphaChannel`, `findIccpChunk`,
  `rewritePngChunks`. They move chunks whole and never recompute a CRC. **T29's `acTL`, `fcTL` and
  `fdAT` are not in `rewritePngChunks`'s whitelist** — an APNG put through it would come out as a
  still image. Widen the set deliberately rather than by accident.
- **`rewritePngChunks` also drops `pHYs`, so PNG output carries no physical-dimension (DPI) record.**
  No requirement asks for one and R-I17's spirit is to strip what the user did not ask to publish, so
  this is deliberate — but it is a behaviour change no design document mentions, and a user who
  relies on a 300 DPI tag for print would notice. ImageMagick does not write `pHYs` for a frame built
  from raw pixels, so today nothing is actually lost; it would matter if a later stage started
  carrying the source's `pHYs` forward. Adding it to the whitelist is a one-word change.
- **T29 (animated PNG)** should take D3's ordering as given — UPNG → apng-optimizer → attach `iCCP`,
  dropping `sRGB` — and can now use `rewritePngChunks` with `{ kind: 'attach' }` to do the attaching,
  which also drops the `sRGB` D3 warns about. Note that T23 measured that ordering on a
  *single-frame* file, not a real APNG. The multi-frame representation animated PNG needs is the one
  GIF will want too, which is why `docs/tasks.md` now runs T29 before T28 — recorded there, not here.
- **`unimplemented` now covers GIF (T28), animated output (T29) and size targeting (T30) only**, plus
  a CICP frame going to JPEG, WebP or PNG. The `JobFailure` kind goes when those land.
- **T30 (size targeting) has a different axis for PNG.** §7.5 already says so. The honest shape is a
  search over `minimum`, but note that below slider 85 the achievable range is narrow: with
  `target: 100` the output is the same file for every `minimum` the image can meet, and the only
  transition is to lossless when it cannot. A PNG size target probably wants `target` as the search
  variable instead, which is a different design from the one shipped here — and the numbers in D8 say
  it will cost quality.
- **Memory.** A PNG job holds the frame (w × h × 4), ImageMagick's copy of it, and libimagequant's
  own copy in a third wasm heap, plus two encoded candidates and oxipng's heap. For a 50 MP frame
  that is roughly 600 MB of pixels across three heaps. Nobody has measured it; C8's figures are for
  *loading* codecs, not running them. Whoever owns §8.2's worker recycling should measure the PNG
  path specifically, because it is the only path with three live heaps.
- **oxipng is single-threaded and will stay so** unless someone sets COOP/COEP (B6, B9). If they do,
  `@jsquash/oxipng` will silently switch to `initThreadPool(navigator.hardwareConcurrency)` inside
  every pipeline worker, and §8.1's budget changes shape.
- **`vite.config.ts` now has an `optimizeDeps.exclude`.** Do not remove it without re-reading the
  comment; the failure it prevents is silent and only appears in dev.
- **Port 5173 is contested.** T26 hit this too. The standard `playwright.config.ts` uses 5173 with
  `reuseExistingServer`, so when another project's dev server holds the port Playwright silently
  reuses it and the whole suite fails against the wrong app — including `app.spec.ts`, which is the
  tell. It was free for most of this task and taken again at the end; the final full-suite run used a
  scratch config on port 5199. Check the port before believing a suite-wide failure.
- **Re-running the harness:** from `verification/t27-png/`, run `pnpm test`, or `T27_QUICK=1 pnpm test`
  for Chromium only with no sweeps and no real files. It needs the repository's root install. The
  real-file half reads `PHOTO_FIXTURES_DIR` and reports `skipped` with a reason when it is unset. It
  records dimensions, counts, sizes, times and metrics only — never names or paths.
  `node render.mjs [dir]` writes the PNGs behind the banding judgement so they can be looked at;
  its output directory is gitignored.

## What could not be proven

Stated plainly, because green tests otherwise imply coverage that does not exist:

- **"No visible banding" is a human judgement and I am not a panel of people.** What is written above
  is: four subjects at slider 10 inspected as images and found clean, plus a numeric step metric that
  agrees, plus the structural argument that the floor aborts rather than degrades. A viewer on a
  wide-gamut display looking at a 16-bit-source sky might still see something none of that catches.
- **Firefox is not proven to honour the ICC profile**, only to carry it byte-identically. Its 2D
  canvas does not colour-manage in this build, so the "the engine applies it" assertion runs on
  Chromium and WebKit only. Firefox's `<img>` rendering — what R-I16's Preview will use — is
  untested here.
- **Firefox's completeness is intermittent.** The committed run processed all twenty-four images on
  all three engines, but an earlier run of the same script dropped two large real files on Firefox
  with "Execution context was destroyed". The cause was not investigated, so a future run may be
  short two rows again.
- **The `EncodeError` for "ImageMagick wrote no `iCCP` for a profiled frame" is unreachable in the
  test set**, so that branch is unexercised. It exists because losing a profile silently is worse
  than failing.
- **`apng-optimizer`'s bundled copy of libimagequant is not compared**, though D8's original Method
  named it. It takes an APNG in and gives an APNG out (A6) and exposes no quantizer, so using it for
  a still PNG would mean assembling a single-frame APNG first.
- **No phone was measured**, and no frame larger than 1600 px on its long edge. Times and memory on a
  50 MP source are unknown for this path.
- **The `imagequant` dev-server finding is specific to Vite 8.3 and this package's layout.** I did
  not check whether other wasm-bindgen "bundler"-target packages in the tree have the same shape —
  none of the other five is that target.
- **Size targeting for PNG (T30) is not designed here**, only observed to be awkward; the note above
  is an observation, not a plan.

## Requirements covered

- **R-Q11:** the slider is remapped in `domain/job.ts` and the mapped value becomes libimagequant's
  `minimum`. The floor of 70 is enforced by the quantizer's abort, so nothing below it can be
  written. The unit tests assert the minimum over the whole slider range is exactly 70 and monotonic;
  the e2e asserts R-Q11's table end to end through the shipped stage.
- **R-Q12:** lossless from slider 85, where the mapping reaches 95 exactly, and not from 84 — proven
  in the unit tests and again end to end, including that the rounding does not move the boundary.
- **R-Q13:** measured. The recommendation is that the floor stays at 70; the evidence is above and in
  D8. `docs/functional-design.md` is unedited — **the user decides.**
- **R-Q14:** both candidates below the lossless point, the smaller kept, a tie kept lossless, through
  the same `pickSmaller` R-Q6 uses. Proven in both directions and on the tie.
- **R-I12:** the profile is carried verbatim on both branches, byte-identical to the source's, and is
  applied by two of the three engines' own decoders.
- **R-I14:** an untagged frame gains no `iCCP`, `sRGB`, `gAMA` or `cHRM`.
- **R-I35:** a CICP frame is refused for PNG unless the signal says no more than an untagged output
  does — the same rule and the same code as JPEG and WebP. This build cannot write PNG `cICP`
  (verified in the binary and empirically).
- **R-I10, R-I17:** structural. The frame has nowhere to put an orientation tag, and the output
  carries only `IHDR`, `PLTE`, `tRNS`, `IDAT`, `IEND` and, where there is one, `iCCP` — asserted
  against a file ImageMagick would otherwise have written with three timestamps in it.
- **R-X46:** the `png-quantized` caveat has a producer for the first time, emitted when the kept
  output is the quantized one.
- **§4.2's PNG-still row, §7.3, §7.4, §7.6:** implemented and measured; the design document is
  updated where the numbers required it.
- **D8:** refuted, with evidence, and the register updated in place.
